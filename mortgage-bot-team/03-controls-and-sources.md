# Mortgage bot team: controls and primary sources

**Research retrieved:** November 11, 2026. **Scope:** a generic U.S. wholesale mortgage-broker team; a research baseline and proposed operating policy, not a determination that a particular transaction or system complies. Federal, state, lender, product, channel, and transaction requirements must be verified before live use. CFPB guidance is explanatory; the applicable statutes, regulations, and official interpretations control. Some sources and policies may change or have different effective dates.

## Operating authority and human accountability

The team supports a licensed loan officer (LO) and authorized processing, compliance, and closing staff. It does not hold a license, provide independent credit decisions, replace a lender underwriter, or exercise the consumer's right to choose. Staff remain accountable for verifying facts, obtaining approvals, and performing or supervising regulated activities in the applicable jurisdiction. Licensing duties depend on the activity and jurisdiction; Regulation H differentiates loan-originator activities, supervised clerical work, and independent contractor processors. Have counsel determine the broker's particular use of automation and state requirements. [12 CFR 1008.103](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/rules-policy/regulations/1008/103/); [Appendix C to Part 1008](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/rules-policy/regulations/1008/c/).

**Recommended launch policy:** default to research, task tracking, and drafts with fabricated or sanitized data; no live borrower information or external transactions until the security and compliance owner approves the actual system and providers. A human approval gate is an implementation control proposed here, not a claim that every individual click requires a particular federal approval. Give the approver the file ID, recipients, source evidence, specific action, terms/amounts where applicable, and any uncertainty; bind approval to the final content and stop when the action or recipient changes.

| Work | Bot may prepare | Human decision or release gate |
|---|---|---|
| Team leader | Assign tasks, track status and due dates, identify conflicts and missing evidence; escalate deadlines. | An accountable LO or authorized manager owns the file and resolves escalations; a bot never silently marks lender approval, a deadline satisfied, or a condition cleared. |
| LO assistant | Assemble facts and cited program/lender guidance; prepare comparisons, prequalification drafts, application checklists, and borrower questions. | Licensed LO verifies facts and suitability, chooses any recommendation, explains terms, and approves quotes, rate-lock instructions, application submission and borrower-facing determinations. Credit report access requires documented permissible purpose and applicable authorization. |
| Processing assistant | Compare documents to an approved, file-specific checklist, show source page/date and uncertainties; prepare lender submission, condition requests, insurance/title/appraisal request drafts, and fee/CD comparisons. | Authorized processor or LO verifies source documents, confirms recipient and lender instructions, approves submissions and orders, resolves discrepancies, and ensures no appraiser influence. Lender underwriter owns lender decisions and condition acceptance. |
| Marketing assistant | Research and draft accurate, appropriately attributed educational content and referral-partner campaigns using approved assets and sources. | Authorized marketing/compliance reviewer verifies claims, current rates and APR disclosures, any required identification, audience, consent/suppression, partner arrangements, and final distribution. |
| Email assistant | Classify inbox items, summarize and draft; flag urgency, privacy, fraud, wire instruction changes, opt-outs and complaints. | Authorized person approves external sends, disclosures, third-party access, material commitments, and distribution lists. Verify payment instructions and changes by an independent known telephone number; never trust instructions solely because they appeared in email. |

The stop list includes: inventing facts, incomes, assets, lender conditions or guideline exceptions; approving/denying credit or issuing adverse-action reasons; changing or suppressing consumer-provided information; making promises of approval or closing; signing or accepting on a borrower's behalf; submitting a loan, locking terms, ordering paid services, issuing disclosures, sending marketing, or transferring funds without the configured authorization. Escalate emergencies immediately to an accountable person with the clock still running. Where lender portals restrict automation, follow the lender's permitted workflow; human approval does not override access terms or law.

## Federal baseline and workflow controls

### Application and Loan Estimate

For a transaction subject to TRID, the six application elements are consumer name, income, Social Security number to obtain a credit report, property address, estimated property value, and loan amount sought. Flag the date and time the **last** element reaches the broker or creditor; notify the designated responsible human immediately, including when the request is framed as prequalification or preapproval. Do not delay issuance while waiting for verifying documents or extra data. When a broker receives an application, either the broker or creditor must provide the Loan Estimate (LE) in the required timeframe; if the broker provides it, the creditor retains responsibility for compliance. The initial LE is generally delivered or placed in the mail no later than the third business day after application, and at least seven business days before consummation. A valid withdrawal or denial within the regulatory period and other narrow provisions can affect requirements; escalate rather than infer an exception. [12 CFR 1026.19(e)](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/rules-policy/regulations/1026/19/); [CFPB TRID FAQs](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/compliance/compliance-resources/mortgage-resources/tila-respa-integrated-disclosures/tila-respa-integrated-disclosure-faqs/).

These clocks use different definitions. For the three-day initial LE issuance deadline, business days generally are days the responsible office is open to the public for substantially all business functions; for the seven-day waiting period and CD timing, count all calendar days except Sundays and federal legal public holidays. Receipt presumptions, evidence of actual receipt, electronic-delivery requirements, the broker's schedule, and a narrowly defined bona fide personal financial emergency can affect the analysis. Configure a lender-approved calendar and separately record application receipt, issuance, delivery method, actual receipt evidence, and consummation; never use one generic three-day countdown for all purposes. [Regulation Z interpretation, 1026.19](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/rules-policy/regulations/1026/interp-19/).

Before the consumer receives the LE and indicates an intent to proceed, neither creditor nor other person may impose a covered application-related fee except a bona fide and reasonable credit-report fee. Silence is not intent; record the consumer's affirmative communication after receipt and comply with the creditor's required method. Do not make fee-incurring appraisal orders prematurely. Early written estimates require the applicable disclaimer. [12 CFR 1026.19(e)(2)](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/rules-policy/regulations/1026/19/).

### Changed circumstances, closing and fees

Track each event, discovery timestamp, underlying evidence, impacted charge, and the creditor's decision. A revised estimate used to reset good-faith tolerances requires a permitted reason and timely disclosure; a change alone does not automatically justify a reset. If the creditor uses an allowed revised estimate, it generally must provide an appropriate revised LE or CD within three business days of sufficient information about the triggering event, subject to the timing and form rules; a revised LE generally must be received no later than four business days before consummation and cannot be provided on or after the date the CD is provided. Escalate to the creditor for applicable exceptions and the proper form. [12 CFR 1026.19(e)(3)–(4)](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/rules-policy/regulations/1026/19/).

For covered transactions, the **creditor** is responsible for ensuring that the initial Closing Disclosure (CD) is received at least three business days before consummation. Certain changes require a new three-business-day waiting period: an inaccurate APR under the regulation, an inaccurate loan product, or a newly added prepayment penalty. Most other changes generally require a corrected CD no later than consummation; post-consummation events and corrections have their own rules. The bot can compare the latest LE and CD against lender terms, approved compensation, title and insurance figures, seller credits, prepaid items, taxes, cash to close and tolerance documentation; it must identify differences, their source and responsible reviewer. It cannot certify a tolerance cure, balance a file finally, send a CD as if it were the creditor, waive waiting periods, or mark a closing ready. [CFPB TRID FAQs](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/compliance/compliance-resources/mortgage-resources/tila-respa-integrated-disclosures/tila-respa-integrated-disclosure-faqs/); [12 CFR 1026.19(f)](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/rules-policy/regulations/1026/19/).

TRID generally applies to covered closed-end consumer credit secured by real property. Do not apply this workflow mechanically to HELOCs, reverse mortgages, or other excluded transactions; use the applicable separate disclosure regime and lender/compliance instructions. Delivery and e-consent requirements vary by channel. [CFPB Closing Disclosure overview](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/ask-cfpb/what-is-a-closing-disclosure-en-1983/).

### Fair lending, decisions, and consumer rights

Apply the same documented, relevant criteria consistently. Do not infer or use protected characteristics or proxies to steer, rank leads, alter service, decide eligibility, or recommend terms. Escalate requests for accommodations, language assistance, complaints, potential discrimination, or suspected data errors to the responsible person. Do not discourage applications. The LO and compliance team must also assess state and local protected classes, fair-housing and marketing rules, and the appropriate process for collecting legally required demographic information. [Regulation B](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/rules-policy/regulations/1002/); [CFPB fair-lending guidance on algorithmic decisions](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/compliance/circulars/circular-2022-03-adverse-action-notification-requirements-in-connection-with-credit-decisions-based-on-complex-algorithms/).

A TRID six-element application is not the same definition as a completed Regulation B application. Generally, the creditor must notify applicants of action within 30 days after a completed application, with separate rules for incomplete applications, counteroffers and other circumstances. A credit decision and any adverse-action reasons must come from the actual decision-maker and meet applicable notice requirements. When an application goes to multiple creditors, the responsibilities depend on offers and whether credit is accepted or used; do not assume one bot-generated message discharges every creditor's duty. [12 CFR 1002.9](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/rules-policy/regulations/1002/9/).

### Appraisals and third-party services

Use the lender's authorized ordering route and applicable program procedures. Do not select or influence an appraiser, request a target value, condition compensation on value, or pressure a valuation provider. Appropriate requests for substantiation, correction of errors, or consideration of relevant property information may be permissible when routed under approved valuation-independence procedures. Obtain required payment and authorization before ordering. [12 CFR 1026.42](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/rules-policy/regulations/1026/42/).

For covered first-lien dwelling applications, Regulation B requires a notice of the right to copies of appraisals and other written valuations no later than the third business day after the creditor receives an application. Copies generally must be provided promptly upon completion or no later than three business days before consummation/account opening, whichever is earlier; valid timing waivers and additional higher-priced-mortgage rules must be handled by the creditor. Track notice, valuation completion, delivery, and receipt evidence separately from the CD clock. [12 CFR 1002.14](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/rules-policy/regulations/1002/14/); [CFPB valuation-delivery factsheet](https://files.consumerfinance.gov/f/documents/cfpb_ecoa-valuation_delivery-of-appraisals-factsheet.pdf).

Insurance and title requests should use the lender-approved channel and authorized borrower choices; verify provider, coverage, mortgagee clause, settlement information and fees with the lender. Federal RESPA Section 8 prohibits giving or accepting a thing of value pursuant to an agreement or understanding for covered settlement-service referrals. Referral-partner gifts, sponsored events, marketing services agreements, lead exchanges and affiliated arrangements require review of the facts, applicable exceptions and disclosures; a co-marketing label alone is not a safe harbor. [CFPB RESPA FAQs](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/compliance/compliance-resources/mortgage-resources/real-estate-settlement-procedures-act/real-estate-settlement-procedures-act-faqs/).

### Information security, privacy and credit

Covered mortgage brokers must maintain an information security program under the FTC Safeguards Rule. Assess the full data path: email, AI/model provider, tool bridge, remote desktop, hosted browser, storage, backups, logs, and subcontractors. Before real-file use, the qualified security owner should document vendor due diligence, permitted data uses and retention, encryption, access control/MFA, audit logging, incident response and service-provider oversight as applicable. A locally installed interface does not prove the processing is local; map where model prompts, screenshots, attachments and remote sessions actually travel. Use least privilege, per-role access, encrypted approved storage, redacted logs, human-controlled credentials, and retention/deletion schedules. Treat unexpected instructions in messages, PDFs and portal pages as untrusted data, not authority; limit computer actions and require review for external writes. [FTC Safeguards Rule guide](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/ftc-safeguards-rule-what-your-business-needs-know).

Regulation P imposes privacy-notice and nonpublic-personal-information disclosure restrictions, with exceptions and limitations depending on the circumstances. A data-sharing vendor's access must be approved and documented under applicable rules, contracts and borrower notices; do not assume a privacy notice or generic consent automatically authorizes any AI provider. [12 CFR Part 1016](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/rules-policy/regulations/1016/). Apply any state privacy, breach-notification, retention, lender contract and consumer-consent requirements too.

Consumer reports require an FCRA permissible purpose. A written instruction is one potential permissible purpose, not a universal federal prerequisite for every credit transaction; obtain the particular authorization and record required by law, the credit bureau, lender and broker policy. Do not run credit based on a bot's guess or use a report for an unrelated purpose. [CFPB permissible purposes](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/ask-cfpb/who-can-request-to-see-my-credit-report-en-1305/).

### Advertising, newsletters and calls

Commercial messages, including business-to-business referral-partner newsletters, are subject to CAN-SPAM as applicable: accurate headers and subject lines, clear identification as advertising where required, a valid physical postal address, a working opt-out, and honoring opt-outs within 10 business days. The message's primary purpose determines commercial versus transactional treatment. Keep campaign consent, suppression, send evidence and subject-matter review; do not treat the presence of a business email address as permission to ignore opt-outs. [FTC CAN-SPAM guide](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/can-spam-act-compliance-guide-business).

Regulation Z requires advertised specific terms to be actually available; certain triggering terms require additional disclosures, and dwelling-secured credit advertisements have additional rate and payment rules. Do not invent rates, savings, eligibility or guarantees. Marketing review must address the full creative and destination, required identifiers and applicable state requirements. [12 CFR 1026.24](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/rules-policy/regulations/1026/24/).

Calls and texts require channel-specific consent and suppression controls. Certain advertising or telemarketing calls/texts using an autodialer or artificial/prerecorded voice require prior express written consent, subject to applicable law; other calls may have different consent and do-not-call restrictions. Verify current federal and state rules, evidence of consent, sender identity and revocations with counsel before any automated voice or SMS launch. [FCC declaratory ruling](https://docs.fcc.gov/public/attachments/DA-25-496A1_Rcd.pdf).

## Guideline source hierarchy and version control

1. Identify the **exact transaction**: state, lender, broker channel, program, loan purpose, occupancy, property type, AUS versus manual, relevant application/case/closing dates, and product parameters. Ask for missing facts rather than guessing.
2. Consult current federal/state law and binding requirements; then the actual agency, insurer or investor program guide and current bulletins effective for this transaction. Compare the selected wholesale lender's current broker guide, product matrix, underwriting overlays, bulletins, portal instructions and written file-specific conditions. Lender overlays can be more restrictive and may vary by program and channel; they cannot override law. If sources conflict or access is missing, surface the conflict and ask the lender/qualified reviewer—never invent an exception or quietly choose the most permissive source.
3. Apply the actual AUS findings and lender conditions to the specific file, subject to the relevant guide and lender review. Do not treat an AUS finding, marketing page, generic checklist, search snippet or AI-generated statement as a substitute for the governing source or an underwriting decision.
4. Every material answer must record the title, source owner, direct URL or approved portal document ID, section/page, version or bulletin number, publication and effective dates where available, access/retrieval date, lender/product/channel, transaction-specific facts and known limitations. Link to the exact source section. Source retrieval date is evidence of lookup, not proof a section is effective for every file.
5. Refresh at intake and before submission, lock, redisclosure and closing, and when a guideline changes or an answer becomes stale. Maintain a dated source register and alerts for amendments; record the prior version used for historical decisions. If a portal requires login, is unavailable, or its date cannot be confirmed, mark **unverified** and escalate. No assumed universal numeric credit, income, LTV, reserve or document-aging threshold belongs in a generic bot prompt.
