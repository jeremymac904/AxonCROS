# Wholesale mortgage lifecycle and document review playbook

**Research baseline:** November 11, 2026. This is a U.S. wholesale broker operating playbook for human-supervised assistants. It describes a federal baseline and representative agency sources; it does not establish legal advice, a lender's current overlay, a specific loan's eligibility, or permission to act in a lender system. State law, the creditor's instructions, transaction type, agency version, effective date, and actual automated underwriting findings matter. Confirm current rules and wholesale lender guidance on each file. Reverse mortgages, HELOCs and other transactions outside ordinary TRID coverage require their own workflows; do not silently use this one.

## Operating contract

The **team leader** owns the file task board, missing-data log, source freshness, deadlines, conflicts, handoffs, and escalation to a named human. The **loan officer assistant** supports intake, scenario research, authorized calculations, borrower communications, and LO review. The **processing assistant** supports document inventory, consistency checks, submission assembly, conditions, service coordination, and closing checklists. The **email assistant** sorts correspondence, drafts requests, extracts tasks, and reconciles sent/received evidence. The **marketing assistant** supports approved, appropriately segmented education and relationship campaigns; it should not access the loan file merely because a contact is a lead. A licensed LO or other appropriately authorized human retains responsibility for advice, communications that require approval, and approvals under the broker's policies. The lender's underwriter makes its underwriting decisions. The creditor remains responsible for duties applicable to it, including applicable disclosure obligations.

In the initial mode, every bot is **research, draft, and review only**: no customer or partner email, credit pull, document upload, registration, lock, submission, fee, disclosure, appraisal, title or insurance order, or portal click with external effect. Do not act simply because a portal button is available. Separate production permissions and explicit approval are needed for each class of action after access, security, vendor terms, lender authority, and supervision have been reviewed. Never claim that a draft, queued task, clicked button, or uploaded file is equivalent to creditor acceptance, customer receipt, underwriting approval, clear to close, funding, or recording.

Each task or recommendation records file ID; task owner; purpose; source document and page or portal event; source date/version; authority and applicability; findings; unresolved conflicts; confidence/limitations; due date; status; and handoff recipient. Record evidence of completed action only after receipt or status is actually verified. Protect the audit trail against changes and restrict it to staff with a need to know.

## Knowledge and evidence routing

1. Identify jurisdiction, transaction purpose, occupancy, property type, product and investor/insurer/guarantor, chosen lender, application and expected closing dates, and underwriting method. If unknown, ask the responsible human or mark the result conditional.
2. Retrieve the currently effective statute/regulation when the issue is legal; the agency or program guide and relevant amendments for the product; the selected lender's current product matrix, overlays, submission and operational instructions; the latest actual AUS findings and lender conditions; and the actual file evidence. Note applicability and effective dates. The creditor and compliance lead resolve conflicts or ambiguity; never assume a stale guide or an agency minimum overrides law or a lender overlay.
3. Cite the exact section and link or authorized internal document, version, effective/retrieval dates, and the affected file fact. Clearly separate an agency rule, an overlay, and a lender condition. Do not quote personal customer data in general knowledge bases. Record when proprietary material is inaccessible or unverified.
4. AUS recommendations and validation findings are case-specific and depend on accurate input and remaining steps. A recommendation is not the lender's credit approval, and a preliminary calculation is not a final qualifying determination. The lender evaluates documentation and resolves contradictions.
5. If a source is missing, a product is outside scope, borrower evidence conflicts, document authenticity is questionable, an expiration may matter, or a calculation is uncertain, mark **needs human review**, explain what is missing, and identify the next evidence needed. Never manufacture facts, signatures, dates, disclosures, orders, approvals, or source access.

## Minimum file inventory and data handling

Use the approved LOS or secure document repository as the system of record; bots should retain only what they need for the task. Use a file ID, document IDs, page references, and redacted account identifiers in shared task queues. Do not store SSNs, complete account numbers, identity documents, tax returns, credit reports, passwords, or sensitive attachments in bot prompts, logs, general marketing systems, or unapproved model services. Confirm approved processing location, vendor retention and training terms, access controls, encryption, retention/deletion, and incident procedures before real file data is processed. Securely request documents through an approved portal rather than asking for sensitive financial information in ordinary email.

Maintain a restricted inventory, with provenance and status, for the information actually needed:

- **Parties and permissions:** borrower/co-borrower identities and contact preferences; authorized representatives; verified consents and permissible purpose for credit and third-party verifications; approved parties and limits on sharing. Do not infer a blanket permission from a contact address or referral.
- **Application and product:** application/receipt timestamps and channel, requested loan amount, purpose, transaction type, proposed terms, intended occupancy, property address and value estimate, sales contract and key deadlines when applicable, selected lender/product, product eligibility assumptions, broker and LO identifiers, and relevant jurisdiction.
- **Financial evidence:** employment and income category, documents and time periods, assets/source of funds, liabilities, credit authorization/report status, other obligations, and reconciliation findings. Limit sensitive values in summaries; preserve original evidence securely.
- **Property and services:** legal description and vesting, title and lien/curative status, taxes/HOA, insurance quote/binder and mortgagee information, flood determination if applicable, appraisal/valuation order and delivery events, inspection or condo information if required.
- **Progress and evidence:** initial and revised LE/CD versions, creditor and delivery/receipt evidence, intent-to-proceed record, dates and deadlines with governing calendar, AUS run and findings, underwriting conditions, lock and expiration, approvals, closing schedule, settlement figures, funding/recording and post-close statuses. A missing timestamp is **unknown**, not the same as completed.

## Stage-by-stage workflow

The steps overlap. A loan can return to an earlier step when a product, borrower circumstance, property, or lender changes. Track each discrete task as pending, ready for review, submitted, acknowledged, completed with evidence, or blocked; do not infer a later state from an earlier one.

### 1. Lead, intake, and prequalification

**Entry:** An individual requests information or a referral is received. **Owner:** LO assistant and named licensed LO. **Checks:** Verify identity/contact method through approved procedure; clarify authorization and intended purpose. Gather only necessary scenario information. Distinguish an unverified borrower estimate, documented fact, and qualifying determination. Explore goals and constraints; identify likely program pathways and current lender overlays without promising approval, a rate, or a fee. Record consent, next steps, and communication preferences. Track the six TRID application elements as they are submitted, not merely when a bot labels the file complete.

**Exit/handoff:** Human LO receives scenario summary, known/unknown facts, potential eligibility questions and exact source references. Once the applicable definition of application is met, team leader opens the disclosure deadline immediately; do not hold the file while collecting a purchase contract or verification documents. The [CFPB explains the six elements](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/ask-cfpb/what-information-do-i-have-to-provide-a-lender-in-order-to-receive-a-loan-estimate-en-1987/).

### 2. Application, creditor selection, and disclosure coordination

**Entry:** Application data is received or a property address completes the six elements. **Owner:** LO assistant monitors; human LO and creditor oversee execution. For covered TRID transactions, application means submission of consumer name, income, SSN to obtain a credit report, property address, estimated property value, and requested mortgage amount. Generally, the creditor must ensure the LE is delivered or placed in the mail no later than the third business day after receiving the application. If a broker receives an application, either the broker or creditor may deliver the LE, but the creditor retains responsibility. A creditor may request more information; it cannot condition the LE on receiving verification documents or additional information. Exceptions and calculation of applicable business days must be checked against the rule and creditor policy. [CFPB TRID FAQs](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/compliance/compliance-resources/mortgage-resources/tila-respa-integrated-disclosures/tila-respa-integrated-disclosure-faqs/).

Review draft registration facts against the application, property, loan terms, pricing, fees, settlement providers and creditor instructions. Check whether a written provider list or other disclosures are required. Surface who is responsible, application receipt and LE due dates, actual LE delivery and borrower receipt, version, any changed circumstances and their dates, and any revised estimate or fee tolerance question. The creditor/compliance reviewer determines disclosure treatment. Do not certify deadlines or deliver disclosures on behalf of the creditor without authority.

**Fee gate:** For transactions covered by the relevant rule, before imposing fees other than a bona fide reasonable credit-report fee, the consumer must have **received** the LE and affirmatively indicated intent to proceed. Record evidence of both; silence is not intent. Ordering or payment is still subject to creditor rules and approval. [Regulation Z §1026.19(e)(2)](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/rules-policy/regulations/1026/19/).

**Exit/handoff:** Responsible human verifies what was delivered, by whom, and on what date; records intent where applicable and routes unresolved issues immediately to the creditor/compliance owner. A generated LE or an email marked sent is not proof of borrower receipt.

### 3. Collect and review supporting documentation

**Entry:** A lawful document request is appropriate for the scenario. **Owner:** processing assistant prepares a tailored list; LO confirms borrower communication. Match collection to program, income and asset types, AUS findings, permitted validation, lender conditions, and effective dates. Use secure channel and deadline-aware follow-up. Never require verification documents as a precondition for providing an LE where prohibited.

**Checks:** Apply the document-review method below. Reconcile source data against application, credit, contract, statements, actual loan terms, underwriting findings, and prior documents. Flag missing pages, stale or unreadable evidence, inconsistent dates or identities, missing authorizations, unexplained cash movements or liabilities, and arithmetic differences. Ask for clarification through an approved request; do not edit originals or fill gaps by inference. Prepare a source-referenced preliminary summary and open questions for the LO/processor.

**Exit/handoff:** Human reviewer has a versioned document index, requirements matrix and exceptions. Conditions depend on the actual lender; an organized file does not imply that a loan is underwritten or eligible.

### 4. AUS, scenario, lender registration and submission preparation

**Entry:** Available information is sufficient for a proposed lender scenario. **Owner:** LO/processing assistants draft; human LO reviews and uses authorized access. Compare product and pricing eligibility, loan purpose, occupancy, property, loan size, documentation, current lender overlays, borrower preferences, compensation arrangements, and valid lock terms. Do not select a lender or present a rate as locked based on stale pricing. When authorized, reconcile the lender registration against source documents and the most recent DU, Loan Product Advisor, GUS, or other applicable findings. Flag changed facts that may require an updated submission, validation or rerun; follow the lender's instructions. Preserve run ID, timestamp, relevant version and every requirement, including property and credit conditions.

**Exit/handoff:** Submission-ready package goes to the designated human with a checklist: application and supporting records; accurate liabilities, qualifying income and funds analysis as preliminary; contract/addenda; AUS findings; relevant disclosures and intent evidence; program-specific documents; condition and conflict log; required forms; and lender-specific submission instructions. The human verifies and authorizes submission. A file uploaded, a registration number, or an AUS recommendation is not a lender approval.

### 5. Lender underwriting and conditions

**Entry:** The lender has acknowledged submission. **Owner:** processing assistant tracks; human LO/processor manages the lender relationship. Capture actual underwriting status and condition list with issuer, date, due date, exact condition text, responsible person, required evidence, and whether the condition is prior-to-approval, prior-to-documents, prior-to-funding, or other lender classification. Do not collapse classifications into a generic checklist. Map each condition to evidence, note contradictions or information gaps, and draft consolidated requests in plain language. Verify that evidence addresses each item before proposing a submission. Track response, lender acceptance, reopening, and revisions separately.

**Exit/handoff:** Only the lender or designated party can mark its conditions satisfied or issue approval/clear to close. A bot can report **uploaded/pending lender review**, citing the receipt. The team leader escalates unresolved critical conditions, deadlines, lock expiration, new debts, altered income, or closing-date risks promptly.

### 6. Title, insurance, appraisal and other third-party work

**Entry:** Appropriate transaction stage and permissions; creditor, buyer and applicable rules determine which services are needed and who selects/orders each one. **Owner:** processing assistant tracks and drafts coordination. Before ordering, verify authorized provider and ordering path, fee gate, lender's loan number and instructions, borrower choice rights, correct property and contact details, and human approval. Obtain order confirmations and due dates; do not represent a draft as an order.

- **Title/settlement:** Reconcile property, parties/vesting, legal description, proposed insured lender and lien priority, existing liens, judgments, taxes, easements or exceptions, payoffs, curative requirements, closing protection where applicable, and lender-required endorsements. Escalate substantive legal/title issues to title, settlement, counsel and lender; do not interpret them conclusively. The Fannie example requires the title policy to insure generally acceptable title and the mortgage's required lien priority: [General Title Insurance Coverage](https://selling-guide.fanniemae.com/sel/b7-2-03/general-title-insurance-coverage).
- **Homeowners and flood insurance:** Coordinate a quote/binder through the borrower's selected agent and creditor instructions. Compare insured name, property, effective date, coverage, deductibles, lender/mortgagee clause, annual premium and payment status against lender requirements. Flag possible flood determination/insurance or condo-master-policy issues and route for qualified review. Do not bind coverage or claim adequacy from an unconfirmed quote.
- **Appraisal/valuation:** Follow creditor and applicable agency-approved order channels, preserve appraisal independence and do not pressure the appraiser or suggest a target value. Fannie places responsibility for selection on the lender and permits use of a third-party vendor subject to its requirements: [Appraiser Selection Criteria](https://selling-guide.fanniemae.com/sel/b4-1.1-03/appraiser-selection-criteria). Check completion, property identity, value, property condition, lender-identified deficiencies and required repairs, not a bot's claim of final acceptability. For covered first-lien applications, Regulation B generally requires copies of each appraisal or other written valuation promptly upon completion or three business days before consummation, whichever is earlier; waivers and other laws have specific constraints. Track actual delivery evidence with creditor: [§1002.14](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/rules-policy/regulations/1002/14/).
- **Other services:** Track tax, HOA, inspection, survey, escrow and closing services where required; confirm appropriate party, receipt, fees, and lender conditions.

**Exit/handoff:** Deliver verified status and outstanding items to the processor and lender. An appraisal receipt, title commitment, or binder is not lender acceptance or clear to close.

### 7. Initial closing disclosure, balancing, and clear to close

**Entry:** Creditor and settlement agent are ready to prepare closing figures. **Owner:** processing assistant compares; creditor/settlement and designated human resolve and issue documents. Compare the most recent applicable LE and all CD versions against executed contract/addenda, lock confirmation, current loan terms, loan amount, lender's fee instructions, title/settlement fee worksheet and payoffs. Review points and lender/broker compensation, lender and seller credits, paid-before-closing items, taxes and prorations, prepaid interest, homeowners/flood premiums, initial escrow, fees and payers, cash to close and funding totals. Flag differences, missing evidence, potential tolerance/cure or changed-circumstance issues, mathematical discrepancies, and whether the lender's written instructions are met. Do not invent a valid change of circumstance or cure; creditor/compliance and settlement agent resolve it.

For covered TRID transactions, the creditor must ensure that the consumer **receives** the initial CD at least three business days before consummation. A corrected CD normally must be provided when terms change; a new three-business-day waiting period is required when the APR becomes inaccurate under the rule, the disclosed loan product becomes inaccurate, or a prepayment penalty is added. Other changes have their own correction requirements; apply creditor guidance and the current rule. Use the applicable definition of business day, delivery method, holidays, and receipt evidence; sending, generating or signing a CD is not automatically proof of timely receipt. [CFPB TRID FAQs](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/compliance/compliance-resources/mortgage-resources/tila-respa-integrated-disclosures/tila-respa-integrated-disclosure-faqs/).

**Exit/handoff:** Present a reconciliation with source references and unresolved differences to creditor and settlement. Report clear to close only when received from the authorized lender, identifying its stated conditions and date. Never promise a closing date or funding based solely on an initial CD.

### 8. Consummation, funding, recording and post-close

**Entry:** Lender has issued its required clearance, timing requirements are satisfied, and settlement is scheduled. **Owner:** human LO/processor coordinates; email assistant drafts approved updates. Verify signer and closing appointment, current figures, lender instructions, conditions due at closing/funding, insurance effective date, payoff instructions, and authorized settlement contact. Do not provide or change wire instructions; direct the consumer to independently verify instructions using a trusted phone number established earlier. See [CFPB closing wire fraud guidance](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/archive/blog/mortgage-closing-scams-how-protect-yourself-and-your-closing-funds/).

Track signing/consummation, rescission where applicable, lender funding approval, actual funding, recording and disbursement separately using evidence from the responsible parties. For applicable refinances, route potential right-of-rescission and waiting-period questions to creditor/compliance; do not apply a blanket rule to all loans. After close, track required corrected disclosures, trailing documents, lender conditions, customer questions, file retention and transfer to servicing as appropriate. Seek only authorized status updates from borrower and partners, keeping personal information restricted.

**Exit/handoff:** Record the confirmed outcome and source for each milestone; mark outstanding trailing/post-close items with owners and due dates. Closing documents signed do not alone establish funded, recorded, or disbursed status.

## Document review method

For each document, produce a structured review: document ID and type; source and time received; pages/period; relevant named party; what was actually observed with page reference; requirement and authority; comparison to application, other documents, and actual lender/AUS condition; missing or conflicting facts; what evidence is needed; and reviewer/decision. Verify legibility and completeness, identity and time period, consistency, basic arithmetic and expiry as applicable. Escalate alterations or authenticity concerns without accusing the borrower; original documents and metadata must be preserved. A document or calculation is not verified merely because a model extracts it.

Use this as a **conditional inventory**, not a universal request list:

| Type | Examples and review focus | Escalate or request more evidence when |
|---|---|---|
| Application and identity | URLA/1003, signed authorizations where required; consistency of parties, residence, occupancy, employment, debts, assets, declarations, and transaction facts. Protect identifiers. | Conflicting identities, missing consent, undisclosed liability, address/date inconsistency, or required field unknown. |
| Wage and employment | Appropriate paystubs, W-2s, employer or authorized third-party verification. Compare employer, pay period, rate/hours, YTD, base vs variable income and continuity. | Overtime, bonuses, commissions, job changes/gaps, fluctuating pay, undocumented income, or contradictory verification; lender determines qualifying income. [Fannie employment standards](https://selling-guide.fanniemae.com/sel/b3-3.1-02/standards-employment-documentation). |
| Self-employment / rental / other | Relevant personal/business tax documents and schedules, profit-and-loss, rental agreements, benefit/award documents or other verification where required. Distinguish gross receipts, expenses, ownership share, stable recurring receipts, and possible tax or accounting questions. | Business ownership or earnings trend unclear; missing schedules, unusual or nonrecurring items, or dependence on continuance. Route calculation to trained human and actual program guidance. |
| Assets and funds to close | Statements or approved verification identifying institution, account holder, period, account identifier and balance, with transaction detail as applicable; reconcile transfers, gifts, liquidation, and available balances. | Missing pages, disputed ownership, borrowed/undisclosed funds, unsupported source, or large deposits requiring documentation under the applicable rule. Under the cited Fannie guide, purchase statements generally cover a most recent full two-month period; alternatives and DU validation can change the documentation path. Fannie distinguishes purchase and refinance treatment of large deposits; do not generalize one rule to every loan. [Asset verification](https://selling-guide.fanniemae.com/sel/b3-4.2-01/verification-deposits-and-assets), [deposit rules](https://selling-guide.fanniemae.com/sel/b3-4.2-02/depository-accounts), [DU validation](https://selling-guide.fanniemae.com/sel/b3-2-02/du-validation-service). |
| Credit and liabilities | Authorized report and current creditor/AUS requirements; reconcile payments, mortgages, undisclosed obligations, recent inquiries, debts to be paid off, and known changes. | Conflicting balances, new debt, disputed tradelines, or possible change in eligibility. Avoid asserting that an inquiry, score, or derogatory item automatically disqualifies. |
| Property and transaction | Contract and all amendments, legal description, appraisal/valuation, title, tax/HOA, condominium and insurance records as applicable. Reconcile price, address, credits, closing date, vesting and repairs. | Contract amendment changes price, credits or closing date; title contains unresolved lien; appraisal differs; insurance details do not match lender requirements. |
| Program-specific | VA certificate of eligibility and applicable VA conditions; FHA case and current Handbook guidance; USDA property/household and lender/GUS/agency conditions; conventional findings and applicable agency guide; product-specific lender overlays. | Missing or inaccessible program/lender rules, unsupported assumption, lender not approved for program, or a source's effective date is uncertain. |
| Disclosures and closing | LE/CD versions, accurate fee worksheets, delivery and receipt evidence, documented intent, lock confirmation, payoffs, settlement statement and funding confirmations. | Unknown receipt, expired lock, changed terms, unbalanced fees, credit or party changes, unverified wire instructions, or ambiguous clearance. |

### Practical review examples

- **Variable earnings:** A paystub's YTD divided by elapsed months may be a useful preliminary consistency check, but it does not establish qualifying overtime, bonus, or commission income. Check the correct program treatment, evidence of history and continuance, and any lender condition; show the human both the calculation and its limits.
- **Transfer between accounts:** A $12,000 outflow in one statement and matching inflow in another are a possible transfer, not proof by themselves. Confirm ownership, dates, amounts, account identifiers and any required transaction trail without exposing full account numbers. Ask the lender whether further documentation is required for that actual program and case.
- **Inconsistent obligation:** If the application shows a $450 payment and a newer credit source shows $600, record both sources and dates, do not overwrite the application silently, and flag the possible effect on preliminary DTI for the LO and underwriter.
- **Price amendment after disclosure:** Record the amendment's date, revised price, credits and closing date; compare to registration, AUS, lock, LE and CD. Promptly route to creditor for eligibility and disclosure treatment rather than automatically calling the amendment a valid tolerance reset.

## Suggested release checks before any production connection

Use synthetic cases to test missing pages, mismatched identity, variable income, document ageing, transfers/large deposits, contradictory AUS findings, expired overlays, a sixth TRID element received on a holiday week, no intent to proceed, initial CD without receipt evidence, changed APR/product, a title lien, a low appraisal, and an email purporting to change wire instructions. Expected result is a traceable finding and a correctly routed human escalation, not an invented resolution. Test access denial, data redaction, retrieval citations, audit-log integrity, approval gates, and safe retry/idempotency with the actual sandbox integrations before expanding permissions. Record who tested, data used, results, fixes, and outstanding gaps; test outcomes do not authorize deployment by themselves.
